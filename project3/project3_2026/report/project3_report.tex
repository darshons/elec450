\section*{Problem, arm model and RTP}
We implemented the Random Tree Planner (RTP) in C++ and planned Panda-arm motions around 3D obstacles. We then compared RTP with OMPL RRT, RRT-Connect and PRM on repeated clutter-scene queries. The seven revolute joint angles define $\mathcal C=\prod_{i=1}^7[q_i^{\min},q_i^{\max}]\subset\mathbb R^7$. The base and gripper are fixed. URDF limits, in radians, are:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}\toprule
joint & 1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\\midrule
min &$-2.9671$&$-1.8326$&$-2.9671$&$-3.1416$&$-2.9671$&$-.0873$&$-2.9671$\\
max &$2.9671$&$1.8326$&$2.9671$&$.0873$&$2.9671$&$3.8223$&$2.9671$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
OMPL uses Euclidean distance and linear interpolation in this bounded joint box. Forward kinematics places 59 robot spheres for collision checks against environment spheres and oriented boxes. Self-collision and dynamic limits are outside the supplied model.

RTP stores states and parent pointers in a vector. Each iteration selects a vertex uniformly, samples a target with goal bias, checks the full motion, and adds only an accepted endpoint. Parent pointers recover an exact goal path or, on timeout, the path to the closest vertex. There is no nearest-neighbor selection or range limit. For Part 2, we rebuilt RTP without changing the algorithm. Tests covered 25 cycles of clearing and reusing a planner, exact and approximate paths, continued solving, starts already at the goal, invalid starts, tree export and state ownership. All allocated states were released when the test objects were destroyed. \texttt{clear()} resets the sampler, frees all states/nodes and clears the goal pointer.

\section*{Experiment design and quantitative comparison}
We used the supplied \texttt{benchmark.py} and \texttt{configs/planners.json} to test all five clutter queries: 20 settings $\times$ 5 queries $\times$ 5 trials = \textbf{500 runs}. A separate 1 s pilot (100 runs, seed 18001) gave 86 exact and 14 approximate outcomes; we then used a 5 s limit for every planner to give the harder settings more time. The comparisons below use only the 500 runs at this limit.

We ran the experiments in the course's \texttt{ompl\_env} environment on macOS ARM64, using Python 3.12.12, OMPL 2.0.2 and AppleClang 17 with a Release build. Trials ran one at a time, with no builds, tests or rendering running alongside them. Normal background OS activity was not controlled. We initialized OMPL once with seed 18002; its random generators then advanced between trials. The recorded seed therefore does not identify an independently seeded trial. Raw OMPL logs, source hashes and software versions are included with the results.

Goal bias $b$ is the probability of sampling the goal. For RTP, we tested .01--.5 to vary how often it attempts to reach the goal. RRT varies range $r=.2,.4,1,2.5$ rad at $b=.05$, and bias $.01,.05,.2$ at $r=1$, separating the two effects. RRT-Connect uses $r=.2,.4,1,2.5,5$ rad, from fine steps to 37\% of the joint-box diagonal. PRM uses $k=5,10,20,40$ neighbors to vary local connectivity. All settings are listed in the planner configuration file.

The collision model, bounds, resolution .005 and edge-clearance sampler stayed fixed. The 13.4165 rad box diagonal gives a nominal maximum checking interval of .0671 rad. We measured paths without simplification or smoothing. Time is OMPL's measured \texttt{solve()} duration, including collision checking. Length is the sum of joint-space edge lengths (rad). Smoothness uses OMPL's \texttt{PathGeometric::}\allowbreak\texttt{smoothness()}. Minimum clearance is the smallest sampled distance from a robot sphere's surface to an obstacle along the path (m), shown below in cm. We did not substitute OMPL's mean waypoint clearance for this minimum.

\section*{Difficulty, hours and contributions}
Exercise 1: difficulty 5/10, about 6 hours. Exercise 2: 6/10, about 7 hours. Exercise 3: 6/10, about 7 hours. The hardest parts were state ownership, choosing obstructed but solvable queries, and explaining why RRT sometimes failed despite finding short paths in other trials.

Darshon implemented and debugged RTP, managed memory, and analyzed performance and algorithm differences. Winson created the scenes and start--goal queries, generated Viser screenshots and saved paths, and configured and ran the benchmarks. Both tested RTP in both scenes, prepared the Part 1 write-up and package, and combined results, figures and explanations for the final report and submission.
